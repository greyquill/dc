The discovery week

What a week of proper discovery produces before anyone writes production code, and how to tell whether it is good enough to build from.

One page, the agenda and the checks.

Three outputs, and nothing else counts as done

The most valuable output is usually not the specification. It is the list of questions nobody had asked, and the discovery that two stakeholders meant different things by the same word.

Five days

Day 1

The outcome

What has to be different in the business, and how anyone would know. Not features.

Day 2

The workflow today

Walked end to end with the people who perform it, including the spreadsheets and the email.

Day 3

Constraints

Systems, data, regulation. The deadline that is real and the one that is aspirational.

Day 4

Options

At least two routes, with what each costs and gives up. Prototype only to settle an argument.

Day 5

Scope and estimate

What is in, what is out, in what order, how long, with what uncertainty.

One week for a contained scope. Two where there are several stakeholder groups or an existing system to understand.

Six questions that earn their place

Most of the value comes from a small number of questions people find slightly uncomfortable.

What happens if we do nothing?If the honest answer is "not much", better to know in week one.
Who has to change how they work?Software needing a behaviour change from people nobody consulted fails regardless of quality.
What was tried before?There is almost always a previous attempt. Why it stalled is the cheapest research available.
What would you drop to ship two months earlier?Produces a real priority order in ten minutes.
Who signs it off, and what will they check?Knowing the acceptance test on day one changes what gets built.
Where does the data come from?Data availability is the commonest reason a plan is rewritten in month two.
A scoring pass on the brief

Before a specification goes to a build team we run it through ClarityAI, our own tool for scoring the clarity and risk of an initiative. It points at the ambiguities, the untestable statements and the missing decisions, line by line. It reliably catches the vague sentence everyone had read past. See ClarityAI.

Four tests on the output

Fail any of these and another day of discovery costs less than the rework it prevents.

What it does not do

Remove uncertaintyIt states it, so it can be priced and planned around.
Fix a price on a vague problemIf the scope is genuinely unclear, the honest output is a smaller first phase, not a bigger number.
Replace the first sprintSome things are only learned by building.
The failure mode

Discovery that runs past two weeks stops producing decisions and starts producing documents. If the questions cannot be closed in the time, narrow the first phase rather than extending the discovery.

Ask these every time

  • What happens if we do nothing?
  • Who has to change how they work?
  • What was tried before, and what happened?
  • What would you drop to ship two months earlier?
  • Who signs it off, and what will they check?
  • Where does the data come from, and who owns it?

Four tests on the output

  • Estimate. Two engineers who were not in the room land in the same range.
  • Acceptance. Every item checkable by someone other than its author.
  • Out of scope. A written list exists. Empty means no boundary was drawn.
  • Disagreement. At least one real one was found and resolved.
Failure mode

Past two weeks, discovery stops producing decisions and starts producing documents. If the questions cannot be closed in the time, narrow the first phase rather than extending the discovery.
